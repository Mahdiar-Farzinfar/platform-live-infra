# Contributor Covenant 3.0 Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We pledge to make this project and its community welcoming, safe, and equitable for everyone who participates.

Although this project is currently maintained by a solo maintainer, it is intended to remain open to future maintainers, contributors, reviewers, and community members.
We are committed to fostering an environment that respects and promotes the dignity, rights, and contributions of all individuals,
regardless of characteristics including race, ethnicity, caste, color, age, physical characteristics, neurodiversity, disability, sex or gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, language, philosophy or religion, national or social origin, socio-economic position, level of education, or other status.

The same privileges of participation are extended to everyone who participates in good faith and in accordance with this Code of Conduct. Contributions may take many forms, including code, documentation, issue reports, reviews, discussions, design proposals, testing, translations, project maintenance, and other constructive forms of participation.

## Encouraged Behaviors

While acknowledging differences in social norms, we all strive to meet this community's expectations for positive behavior. We also understand that our words and actions may be interpreted differently than we intend based on culture, background, or native language.

With these considerations in mind, we agree to behave mindfully toward each other and act in ways that center our shared values, including:

1. Respecting the **purpose of this project**, its technical goals, its activities, and its ways of collaboration.
2. Engaging **kindly, professionally, and honestly** with other participants.
3. Respecting **different viewpoints, experiences, technical approaches, and levels of familiarity** with the project.
4. **Taking responsibility** for our actions, communications, and contributions.
5. Gracefully giving and accepting **constructive, relevant, and actionable feedback**.
6. Committing to **repairing harm** when it occurs.
7. Respecting the time, attention, and boundaries of the maintainer and other contributors.
8. Following the project's documented contribution, review, security, and communication processes.
9. Behaving in other ways that promote and sustain the **well-being and long-term health of the project community**.

## Restricted Behaviors

We agree to restrict the following behaviors in this project community. Instances, threats, and promotion of these behaviors are violations of this Code of Conduct.

1. **Harassment.** Violating explicitly expressed boundaries or engaging in unnecessary personal attention after any clear request to stop.
2. **Character attacks.** Making insulting, demeaning, or pejorative comments directed at a maintainer, contributor, reviewer, community member, or group of people.
3. **Stereotyping or discrimination.** Characterizing anyone's personality or behavior on the basis of immutable identities or traits.
4. **Sexualization.** Behaving in a way that would generally be considered inappropriately intimate in the context or purpose of this project community.
5. **Violating confidentiality.** Sharing or acting on someone's personal, private, or confidential information without their permission.
6. **Endangerment.** Causing, encouraging, or threatening violence or other harm toward any person or group.
7. **Trolling, bad-faith disruption, or deliberate obstruction.** Repeatedly disrupting discussions, reviews, issue tracking, or project operations in a manner that prevents constructive collaboration.
8. **Abuse of project infrastructure.** Using issues, pull requests, discussions, communication channels, automation, or other project resources to harass, threaten, spam, manipulate, or otherwise harm participants.
9. Behaving in other ways that **threaten the safety, dignity, or well-being** of this project community.

### Other Restrictions

1. **Misleading identity.** Impersonating someone else for any reason, or pretending to be someone else to evade enforcement actions.
2. **Failing to credit sources.** Not properly crediting the sources of content, code, documentation, designs, or other materials you contribute.
3. **Promotional materials.** Sharing marketing, commercial, fundraising, recruitment, or other promotional content in a way that is outside the norms of the project or its communication channels.
4. **Irresponsible communication.** Failing to responsibly present content that includes, links to, or describes any other restricted behaviors.
5. **Unsolicited private contact.** Contacting the maintainer or other participants privately in a way that bypasses established project channels, ignores stated boundaries, or creates pressure to respond.
6. **Manipulation of project processes.** Misrepresenting facts, fabricating reports, submitting knowingly misleading contributions, or otherwise attempting to manipulate review, moderation, release, or decision-making processes.

## Reporting an Issue

Tensions can occur between community members even when they are trying their best to collaborate. Not every conflict represents a Code of Conduct violation, and this Code of Conduct reinforces encouraged behaviors and norms that can help avoid conflicts and minimize harm.

When an incident does occur, it is important to report it promptly. To report a possible violation, please contact the current maintainer at:

**[Google Form](https://forms.gle/aj4LAibHncV3SQKD8)**

Because this project is currently maintained by a solo maintainer, reports may initially be received and reviewed by that maintainer. As the project grows, responsibility for receiving, reviewing, or addressing reports may be delegated to trusted maintainers or designated community representatives. Any such delegation should preserve confidentiality, impartiality, and the safety of everyone involved.

Reports should include enough information to understand what happened, where it happened, who was involved, and any relevant links or supporting material. Please avoid sharing sensitive personal information unless it is necessary for reviewing the report.

The maintainer, or designated representatives acting on behalf of the project, will take reports seriously and make reasonable efforts to acknowledge and respond to them in a timely manner.
Reports will be reviewed as fairly and confidentially as practical. The review may include examining messages, issue and pull request history, logs, recordings, or other relevant material, as well as communicating with witnesses and other participants.

Investigation and enforcement actions will generally be handled privately with the involved parties. Information may be communicated to the wider community only when necessary, appropriate, and consistent with the safety and confidentiality of those involved.

## Addressing and Repairing Harm

The following enforcement ladder is a guideline for addressing violations of this Code of Conduct. The appropriate response will depend on the severity, context, impact, and history of the incident. Depending on the circumstances, lower steps may be skipped.

When the project has more than one active maintainer or designated community representative, enforcement decisions may be reviewed or handled by more than one person whenever practical. The current maintainer may use reasonable judgment when an immediate action is necessary to protect participants or project infrastructure.

1) Warning  
   1) Event: A violation involving a single incident or series of incidents that can reasonably be addressed through clarification and corrective action.  
   2) Consequence: A private, written warning from the maintainer or designated project representative.  
   3) Repair: Examples of repair include a private written apology, acknowledgement of responsibility, correction or removal of harmful content, and seeking clarification on applicable expectations.

2) Temporarily Limited Activities  
   1) Event: A repeated instance of a violation that previously resulted in a warning, or the first instance of a more serious violation.  
   2) Consequence: A private, written warning with a time-limited cooldown period. Access to particular project activities, such as issue comments, pull requests, discussions, reviews, or direct interactions with specific participants, may be temporarily limited.  
   3) Repair: Examples of repair may include making an apology, correcting affected contributions, using the cooldown period to reflect on actions and impact, and being thoughtful about re-entering project spaces after the period is over.

3) Temporary Suspension  
   1) Event: A pattern of repeated violations that previous interventions have not resolved, or a single serious violation.  
   2) Consequence: A private written notice with conditions for return from suspension. Temporary suspension may include removal or limitation of access to project repositories, communication channels, issue trackers, contribution workflows, or other project resources.  
   3) Repair: Examples of repair include respecting the suspension, meeting the specified conditions for return, correcting relevant harm, and being thoughtful about how to reintegrate with the project when the suspension is lifted.

4) Permanent Ban  
   1) Event: A pattern of repeated Code of Conduct violations that other steps have failed to resolve, or a violation so serious that the maintainer or designated project representatives determine there is no reasonable way to keep the community safe with this person as a participant.  
   2) Consequence: Access to project repositories, tools, communication channels, contribution workflows, and other community spaces is removed. Permanent bans should be used rarely, should have strong reasoning behind them, and should be applied only when necessary to protect the project community or infrastructure.  
   3) Repair: There is no possible repair within the project community in cases of this severity.

This enforcement ladder is intended as a guideline. It does not limit the ability of the maintainer, future maintainers, or designated community representatives to use their discretion and judgment in keeping with the best interests of the project and its community.

Enforcement decisions should be based on behavior and impact, not on a participant's role, seniority, popularity, employment, or relationship with the maintainer. The project may also take immediate technical or administrative measures when necessary to protect project infrastructure, data, or participants.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all project and community spaces, including but not limited to:

- The source repository and its issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, and comments.
- Official project communication channels, mailing lists, chat rooms, and websites.
- Project-related events, meetings, and online or offline gatherings.
- Interactions involving project accounts, automation, infrastructure, or official communication tools.
- Private communication when it is directly connected to project participation or when it is used to harass, threaten, or harm another project participant.

This Code of Conduct also applies when an individual is officially representing the project in public or other spaces. Examples of representing the project include using an official project email address or account, posting through an official project social media account, speaking on behalf of the project, or acting as an appointed representative at an online or offline event.

As the project evolves, the maintainer may establish additional maintainers, reviewers, moderators, or other community roles. Those roles do not replace this Code of Conduct; they help apply it consistently and transparently as the community grows.
